Pool of Radiance · DOS port · Tandy 1000 sound

The game's eleven sounds, redesigned for the Tandy chip

Every sound Pool of Radiance plays, as its own Tandy driver plays it, beside the same tune with one driver fault fixed and a new sound designed from what it is for. All three go through the same emulation of the SN76489 at the same scale, so a louder clip is louder on the chip too.

Original: the game's own driver, as the game plays it Noise fixed: the same tune, noise register written only when it changes Designed: a new script for the chip
Spectrograms: 60 Hz to 12 kHz, log scale, one level scale for every clip.

What the capture showed

What carried over from Eye of the Beholder

  • Design from use, not from the original's sound. The EOB listening passes kept 110 designed sounds of 117; mapping the AdLib's registers to the chip won only the two pieces of music.
  • Write the chip state once a tick as a script, and write only what changed. EOB's designs stay under about 360 bytes a second; these stay under 700.
  • The same building blocks: one tick of noise for an attack, periodic noise clocked by tone 2 for thumps under the chip's 109 Hz floor, noise swept with tone 2 for whooshes, vibrato after the attack, a delayed second tone for an echo.
  • Fit the time the game gives a sound. EOB's mixer let effects ring; here a third of the sounds get a tenth of a second, so those designs say everything in 80 to 95 ms.
  • Listen in passes. Each card has a pick and a note; the box at the end collects them as JSON for the next pass, as EOB's picker did.

The sounds, most heard first

Tunes nothing plays

The driver's tables hold 20 tunes. The game calls PlaySound only with 2 to 12 (tunes 1 to 11), so tunes 12 to 20 are never heard. Tune 20 is silent on the Tandy chip. They are here for completeness and get no design.
